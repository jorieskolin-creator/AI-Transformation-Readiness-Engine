ToolFirst Ltd — AI Tool Adoption Status

The organization has purchased several generative AI tools and encourages employees to experiment. There is no documented AI operating rhythm, no formal demand intake, and no decision-rights matrix for AI work. Business teams submit ideas through email or directly to the data team.

AI initiatives are treated as normal IT projects even when requirements are uncertain. Two chatbot pilots were announced as successes because demos were popular, but neither pilot reached production, service ownership, measurable adoption, or customer impact measurement. Lessons were not converted into reusable patterns.

The data science team owns most AI knowledge. Prompt patterns are kept in personal notes, and failures are discussed informally. There is no community of practice, incident review, or shared playbook.

AI is mostly used to summarize documents and speed individual tasks. Roles, handoffs, exception paths, and human review points have not been redesigned. Managers still measure activity volume and utilization. Employees report extra checking work because AI outputs often need correction.

There is no model gateway, model registry, prompt versioning, evaluation suite, AI observability, drift monitoring, or rollback process. Sensitive documents are sometimes copied into experimentation tools after manager approval by email. Risk classification and responsible AI controls are planned for next year.

The AI strategy presentation says AI is important for competitiveness, but it does not define where not to use AI. Use cases are selected because they are visible or easy to demo. No impact statements, baselines, portfolio kill criteria, service blueprints, or value stream maps are attached.
