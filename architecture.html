AI Transformation Assessment Engine — Evidence-Gated Scanner Architecture

The AI Transformation Engine is not an AI tool checklist. It is a structured analytical pipeline for testing whether an organization can sense, decide, design, deliver, govern, learn, and scale AI-enabled value creation safely. AI readiness is organizational and architectural readiness, not tool readiness.

The engine evaluates actual evidence: operating rhythm, decision rights, service-area ownership, AI lifecycle control, semantic data context, value framing, governance, safety, learning loops, and business architecture. Plans and slogans score low. Tool adoption alone does not prove readiness. Pilots become meaningful only when they are tied to value, safety, learning, and repeatable scaling.

The design reuses the proven evidence-gated scanner mechanics: local parsing, parse-quality checks, DLP scanning, five parallel forensic audit batches, independent evidence verification, targeted rescans, deterministic metrics, confidence-gated synthesis, fact-checking, sanitation, and a GO/WARN/BLOCK quality gate.

AI readiness is the organization’s ability to turn AI into measurable value through adaptive operating models, reliable architecture, governed data, accountable service ownership, and safe learning loops.
01 How the Engine Thinks

The Forensic Analysis Pipeline

The pipeline is deliberately constrained. The scanner extracts candidate evidence. The verifier checks whether the source material really supports it. The mathematics layer scores only verified data. The synthesizer receives validated metrics as hard boundaries. The fact-checker and sanitation layer then remove unsupported claims before the report is shown.

Phase 0 — Pre-Flight
Local parsing · DLP scan · parse-quality metadata · relevance warnings
Client-side parsers extract source material before model analysis. PDF text is extracted with page markers, sparse or visual PDF pages can be attached as visual evidence, tables are normalized into summaries, HTML is sanitized, and JSON is normalized. A DLP pass plus deterministic redaction catches secrets and sensitive identifiers. Low AI-transformation keyword signal is a warning when material is safe and parseable, not a hard rejection.
Phase 1 — Forensic Audit
5 parallel AI calls · claude-sonnet-4-6 primary · A-E batches
Five batches run simultaneously. Each batch evaluates 10 criteria: 5 maturity indicators and 5 opposing anti-patterns. Each criterion has 3 evidence questions scored on a 0-3 scale. Every captured quote is tagged with one evidence category so the report can distinguish policy evidence from operational, architecture, data, governance, learning, or value evidence.
Batch AAdaptive Operating Model
Batch BEnterprise AI Architecture & Platform Readiness
Batch CAI Strategy, Governance & Value Realization
Batch DData Foundations, Ownership & Accessibility
Batch EBusiness Capability & Service Architecture
Total: 50 criteria · 150 evidence questions · 10-category evidence taxonomy · structured JSON output.
Phase 1.5 — Evidence Check
gemini-3.1-pro-preview · independent verifier · targeted rescan budget
Each completed A-E batch is checked against the raw source material before Phase 2 calculates metrics. The verifier classifies every item as supported, weak, unsupported, or missing. Anti-pattern absence is separately labeled as confirmed present, partial finding, tested absent, or not assessed. Weak or over-scored criteria can trigger targeted rescans; the engine now budgets those rescans toward the highest-value evidence gaps instead of chasing every weak signal.
Phase 2 — Mathematical Validation
No AI · deterministic scoring · evidence firewall
This is the firewall between evidence extraction and strategy. Evidence-checked scores are processed through deterministic arithmetic to produce readiness, maturity depth, anti-pattern burden, anti-pattern clearance, anti-pattern coverage, delivery integrity, evidence density, domain scores, confirmed findings, verified absences, not-assessable anti-patterns, and silent areas. Sparse evidence caps the result.
Phase 3 — Confidence-Gated Synthesis
sonnet summary · opus roadmap · LOW-confidence fast path
Outputs depend on confidence. Summary and diagnosis are separated from roadmap planning. HIGH and MEDIUM confidence can produce planning and a four-phase roadmap. LOW confidence deliberately returns findings mode only: evidence-backed findings, missing evidence, validation plan, NO_GO, and an empty directive roadmap. This avoids spending time and trust on advice the evidence cannot support.
Fact-Check, Sanitation & Quality Gate
gpt-5.5 · independent validation · GO / WARN / BLOCK
GPT-5.5 checks whether claims can be traced to source evidence or validated metrics. Unsupported strategy fragments are removed, rewritten, quarantined, or surfaced in the hygiene appendix. The quality gate then aggregates delivery integrity, evidence density, grounding, fact-check status, and sanitation results into a visible GO/WARN/BLOCK decision.
Interactive Dashboard + Standalone HTML Export
Final deliverable · persona switcher · findings mode · source coverage gaps
The artifact includes the scorecard, domain breakdown, evidence check section, source coverage gaps, anti-pattern semantics, persona-specific summaries, roadmap or findings mode, and quality appendix. The report ships with its own audit trail attached.

Why This Order Matters

The forensic auditor extracts evidence; it does not recommend. This prevents confirmation bias where the system scores evidence higher because a strategy would be attractive.

The evidence checker treats scanner output as provisional. Unsupported claims are downgraded before deterministic scoring can see them.

Phase 2 mathematics cannot be charmed by prose. Readiness is capped by evidence density and anti-pattern absence is positive only when tested.

The Knowledge Base is not customer evidence. It can inform rubric language and roadmap tactics, but it cannot prove the customer’s current state.

The strategy writer cannot rewrite the diagnosis. It receives locked metrics and must stay inside the confidence bracket.

The model that drafts the roadmap does not grade its own work. Independent validation and sanitation sit after synthesis.

02 Roles and Guardrails

Three Personas. One Evidence Contract.

The report has three executive lenses, but the facts must agree. The Transformation Lead needs operating-model truth. The CIO/CTO or CDAO needs architecture, platform, data, and governance implications. The Business / Service Area Owner needs impact, adoption, ownership, and value-stream consequences.

Forensic Auditor

  • Extract explicit proof of AI transformation capability from the submitted material.
  • Do not give the benefit of the doubt. Plans, slogans, and ambitions score 1 at most.
  • Tool presence is not readiness. A model, platform, chatbot, or pilot must show operating proof.
  • Silence is data. If a document is silent on a criterion, score the criterion 0 and name the absence.
  • Document-type signatures matter. A narrow policy is allowed to be silent on architecture, data, or value realization.

Transformation Strategist

  • Use Phase 2 metrics and checked findings as the source of truth.
  • Do not invent scores, numbers, capabilities, owners, maturity, or business impact.
  • Use the Knowledge Base only for roadmap mechanisms and tactic IDs, never as current-state proof.
  • LOW confidence means findings mode: no directive roadmap, no tactic prescriptions, and planning decision NO_GO.
  • Use active language for allowed roadmap actions: establish, implement, test, route, govern, integrate, measure.
The engine is intentionally strict because AI transformation self-assessments are usually over-optimistic. Organizations often have tools, pilots, and ambition before they have ownership, data meaning, release control, service architecture, or value proof.
03 Dual Streams

Two Streams. One Diagnosis.

Every domain has two opposing streams. Stream A measures maturity: evidence of capability, ownership, operating proof, architecture, governance, data quality, learning, and value realization. Stream B measures anti-patterns: evidence of friction, fragility, decision fog, brittle integration, context-poor data, unsafe autonomy, or tool adoption without value proof.

High Maturity + Low Anti-Patterns

Genuinely adaptive only when anti-pattern coverage is high and absence has been meaningfully tested.

High Maturity + High Anti-Patterns

Structured but inconsistent. The organization has mechanisms, but friction blocks scale or trust.

Low Maturity + High Anti-Patterns

Transformation work is needed before AI-enabled value creation can scale safely.

Low Maturity + Low Anti-Patterns

Usually insufficient evidence unless the absence of anti-patterns was directly tested by source material.

Anti-pattern absence is positive only when verified. A zero anti-pattern score can mean tested absent, which is useful evidence of clearance, or not assessed, where the source simply did not cover the issue. Low burden with low coverage means unknown, not excellent.

04 The Domain Pack

The 50-Criteria AI Transformation Knowledge Base

Five domains. Each domain holds 5 maturity indicators paired against 5 anti-patterns. Each criterion carries 3 evidence questions. The result is a 50-criterion, 150-question scanner for whether AI can become safe, measurable, service-owned value creation.

Stream A · Batch A · Adaptive Operating Model

Maturity Indicators

  • A1 — AI Operating Rhythm & Decision Rights: strategy, demand intake, delivery, learning, and governance are connected through clear cadence and ownership.
  • A2 — Adaptive AI Demand Routing: predictable, iterative, exploratory, and transformative work are routed through appropriate delivery paths.
  • A3 — Evidence-Based Launch-and-Learn: Kickstart validation, bounded vertical slices, and learning loops feed Building-the-System scaling.
  • A4 — Shared Learning Architecture: communities, chapters, guilds, incidents, and pilot lessons create reusable competence.
  • A5 — Human-AI Work Redesign: roles, handoffs, review points, and human-in-the-loop responsibilities are redesigned around higher-value work.
Stream B · Batch A · Adaptive Operating Model

Anti-Patterns

  • AI decision fog and governance fat.
  • One-size-fits-all AI delivery.
  • Pilot purgatory without production, ownership, or value proof.
  • Fragmented AI knowledge and expert-dependent hero culture.
  • Digital Taylorism and workslop that speed fragments while degrading the whole system.
Stream A · Batch B · Enterprise AI Architecture & Platform Readiness

Maturity Indicators

  • B1 — Service-Boundary-Based AI Integration: brownfield systems are wrapped through stable APIs, events, connectors, and documented failure modes.
  • B2 — AI Lifecycle & Release Control: models, prompts, agents, tools, datasets, configurations, and evaluations are versioned and releasable.
  • B3 — AI Observability & Evaluation System: production AI is monitored for quality, safety, cost, latency, groundedness, drift, and business impact.
  • B4 — Secure-by-Design AI Trust & Safety Layer: red teaming, guardrails, access controls, escalation, and Agent Behavioral Contracts are embedded.
  • B5 — AI Platform as Product: shared AI services, model gateways, deployment templates, and developer experience are managed as reusable platform products.
Stream B · Batch B · Enterprise AI Architecture & Platform Readiness

Anti-Patterns

  • Legacy labyrinth and brittle AI connectivity.
  • Notebook-to-production or prompt-to-production chaos.
  • Black-box AI operations without evaluation or drift detection.
  • Safety theater and unbounded autonomy.
  • Tool fragmentation and hidden factory platform work.
Stream A · Batch C · AI Strategy, Governance & Value Realization

Maturity Indicators

  • C1 — Purpose-Driven AI Strategy: AI ambition is tied to strategy, customers, values, business model, and where-not-to-use boundaries.
  • C2 — Impact-Driven AI Value Framing: initiatives start from Impact Statements, baselines, value hypotheses, and post-release measurement.
  • C3 — Embedded AI Governance Model: governance enables safe value creation inside service, platform, security, data, and business ownership.
  • C4 — Risk-Based Responsible AI Control: use cases are classified by risk, autonomy, data sensitivity, user impact, and regulatory exposure.
  • C5 — Evidence-Based AI Investment Portfolio: kill, continue, pivot, and scale decisions use impact, risk, readiness, cost, and learning evidence.
Stream B · Batch C · AI Strategy, Governance & Value Realization

Anti-Patterns

  • AI slogan strategy without strategic choices.
  • Use-case chasing and vanity benefits.
  • Rigid gatekeeping governance that slows flow without improving safety.
  • Unclassified risk and ambiguous accountability.
  • AI investment drift, sunk-cost persistence, and platform spend without value validation.
Stream A · Batch D · Data Foundations, Ownership & Accessibility

Maturity Indicators

  • D1 — Domain-Owned AI Data Products: service areas or domains own AI-critical data quality, meaning, lifecycle, and usability.
  • D2 — Context-Rich AI-Ready Data: data includes semantic context, service meaning, process linkage, and decision relevance.
  • D3 — Data Quality & Lineage Control: training, retrieval, inference, and decision data are cataloged, versioned, freshness-monitored, and traceable.
  • D4 — Governed AI Data Access: classification, purpose limitation, privacy, masking, retention, and boundaries are built into AI data flows.
  • D5 — Reusable AI Data Access Patterns: RAG, embeddings, search, feature, batch, real-time, and event-stream patterns are governed and reusable.
Stream B · Batch D · Data Foundations, Ownership & Accessibility

Anti-Patterns

  • Ownerless data and context loss.
  • Raw data without meaning.
  • Data swamp, broken lineage, stale copies, and manual files.
  • Data leakage and over-broad AI access.
  • Ad hoc retrieval and duplicate knowledge stores.
Stream A · Batch E · Business Capability & Service Architecture

Maturity Indicators

  • E1 — AI-Anchored Service & Capability Architecture: AI opportunities connect to service catalogs, capability maps, customer paths, and value streams.
  • E2 — AI-Ready Service Blueprinting: services are mapped end-to-end across customer journey, frontstage, backstage, systems, data, and handoffs.
  • E3 — Traceable AI Solution Structure: AI features trace from customer need to capability, component, data, platform, and work package.
  • E4 — Service Area Ownership of AI Value Creation: integrated teams own business, application, data, AI outcomes, and continuous improvement.
  • E5 — Phased AI Scaling Through Service Areas: rollout is sequenced by readiness, architecture, data, risk, and reusable learning patterns.
Stream B · Batch E · Business Capability & Service Architecture

Anti-Patterns

  • AI ideas detached from business architecture.
  • Spot optimization and silo automation.
  • Untraceable AI build logic.
  • Disconnected AI project teams.
  • Big-bang AI transformation without absorption readiness.
05 Evidence and Scoring

0-3 Evidence Scale and AI Maturity Ladder

The scanner asks what the submitted material proves. A statement that “AI is strategic” is not enough. A pilot is not enough. A platform purchase is not enough. Higher scores require operating proof, measured feedback loops, tested controls, ownership records, or traceable production behavior.

0
Absent

No evidence found. The source is silent or not assessable for that criterion.

1
Aspirational

Intent, buzzwords, plans, pilots being explored, or future-tense claims without operating proof.

2
Operational

Functioning behavior, process, ownership, review, or delivery practice is described.

3
Embedded

Mechanisms, enforcement, automation, measurement, release control, and learning loops are visible.

Readiness Classification

Insufficient evidenceblocked classification
The source material does not support a directive maturity conclusion. The report should name missing evidence and return findings mode.
Emergingearly capability
The organization shows intent, early pilots, or isolated mechanisms, but lacks enough operating proof to show repeatable AI value creation.
Structureddefined mechanisms
Core structures exist: ownership, governance, platform, data practices, and service framing are visible but not yet fluid or scaled.
Scaling with frictioncapability plus blockers
The organization has meaningful AI transformation capability, but anti-pattern burden or uneven service-area absorption blocks smooth scaling.
Adaptive / Value-creatinghigh evidence, low tested burden
AI-enabled value creation is service-owned, architecturally integrated, data-grounded, governed, measured, and continuously improved.

Evidence Categories

The score asks how strong the evidence is. The category tag asks what kind of evidence it is. Every evidence quote is tagged with exactly one category: Policy, Process, Operational, Automation, Accountability, Architecture, Data, Governance, Learning, or Value.

This matters because a company can have extensive policy evidence and almost no operating evidence. Another can have platform automation but no service ownership. The category footprint makes those differences visible.
06 Submission Guidance

What to Submit

The assessment accepts any combination of documents. More document types provide richer signal across all five domains. Absence of entire document categories is itself a maturity signal. Do not manufacture missing documents for the assessment; the absence is part of the diagnosis.

Accepted Setsource material
Strategy documents, AI governance standards, architecture diagrams, data ownership models, platform lifecycle standards, pilot reviews, service blueprints, value stream maps, risk registers, model/prompt release records, red-team reports, and AI value realization reviews.
Local Parsingnot model parsing
PDF, HTML, CSV/TSV, JSON, and image inputs are parsed or encoded locally before analysis. Models analyze extracted material; they are not the primary parsers.
Visual Evidenceselected pages
Sparse-text or dashboard-like PDF pages can be included as images so visible diagrams, tables, operating models, service maps, and evidence screenshots are not lost.
Reference Knowledge Baserubric and tactics context
The remote Knowledge Base can provide methodology context and approved tactics. It is never treated as proof of the assessed organization’s current state.

Useful Evidence Types

  • AI operating model, decision-rights map, or governance cadence.
  • AI strategy with explicit value hypotheses and where-not-to-use boundaries.
  • Portfolio review materials showing kill, continue, pivot, or scale decisions.
  • Service blueprints, value-stream maps, or capability maps tied to AI opportunities.
  • Service-area ownership model for business, application, data, and AI outcomes.
  • AI platform lifecycle, model/prompt registry, evaluation, rollback, or observability records.
  • Data product ownership, semantic definitions, lineage, access, privacy, and retrieval-quality evidence.
  • Responsible AI risk classification, human escalation, red-team, and Agent Behavioral Contract artifacts.
  • Pilot retrospectives showing Launch-and-Learn feedback converted into reusable playbooks.
  • Value realization dashboards showing impact, quality, flow, adoption, safety, and learning outcomes.
07 Deterministic Metrics

The Firewall Between AI and Roadmap

Phase 2 contains no generative AI. It applies deterministic arithmetic to evidence-checked audit logs. Unsupported scanner output has already been rescanned or downgraded before this layer runs.

Evidence-Gated Readiness0-100, capped
readiness = maturity_depth - anti_pattern_penalty + tested_clearance_bonus then capped by evidence density and delivery integrity
The headline score starts from validated maturity, subtracts confirmed anti-pattern burden, rewards only tested clearance, and caps optimistic scores when source evidence is sparse.
Maturity Depth0-100
maturity_depth = (sum of 25 maturity scores / 75) * 100
Average maturity across all five domains. Captures partial progress that a simple “fully mature criteria” count would miss.
Anti-Pattern Burden0-100
burden = (sum of confirmed or partial anti-pattern scores / 75) * 100
Average severity across harmful evidence. Higher means more friction blocking safe AI-enabled value creation.
Anti-Pattern Clearance0-100
clearance = (tested absent anti-pattern criteria / 25) * 100
How much of the anti-pattern surface was meaningfully tested and found absent. This is the only way absence becomes positive evidence.
Anti-Pattern Coverage0-100
coverage = (tested_absent + confirmed_present + partial_finding) / 25 * 100
Separates “we found no problems” from “the submitted material did not test the problems.”
Evidence Density0-100
evidence_density = criteria with source evidence or verified absence / 50 * 100
Measures whether the source actually supports the assessment, not merely whether the pipeline returned data.
Domain BreakdownA-E scores
domain_score[batch] = sum of 5 maturity scores in that domain (0-15)
Shows whether readiness is concentrated in governance, platform, data, operating model, or service architecture.
08 Strategy and Roadmap

Strategy Synthesis Output

Phase 3 produces structured JSON consumed by the dashboard. It cannot fabricate optimism because the visual scorecard, classification, evidence density, domain scores, anti-pattern findings, and source gaps are locked by deterministic validation.

Kickstart

Early roadmap phases diagnose readiness, select a service-area domain, validate a bounded vertical slice, test value and safety, and convert pilot learning into a playbook. This is where Impact Statements, Launch-and-Learn, Secure-by-Design, and evidence-backed business cases become practical.

Building the System

Later roadmap phases integrate architecture, platform, data products, service teams, operating rhythm, embedded governance, AI lifecycle management, Sense & Respond loops, and scaled learning across value streams.

What Makes Phase 3 Trustworthy

  • The visual scorecard and readiness classification come from Phase 2. The synthesizer cannot change them.
  • Persona summaries differ by lens, not facts.
  • Evidence summary and diagnosis are separate from roadmap synthesis.
  • Roadmap actions must cite valid tactics only when the Knowledge Base problem pattern matches the locked finding.
  • Generic evidence-gathering or validation actions may omit tactic IDs; no tactic ID is better than a wrong tactic ID.
  • LOW-confidence reports return findings mode and an empty roadmap.
  • No fabricated numbers: any percentage in prose must correspond to Phase 2 metrics.
09 Model Routing

Model Roles and Fallback Chains

Model routing is centralized in src/models.ts. Each stage has a primary model and an ordered fallback chain. The design avoids lightweight fallbacks for this engine and prefers GPT-5.5, Claude Sonnet/Opus, and Gemini Pro-class models when fallback is needed.

Pre-flight

Primary: gemini-3.5-flash. Fallback: gemini-2.5-pro, then gpt-5.5 low reasoning.

Forensic audit

Primary: claude-sonnet-4-6. Fallback: gpt-5.5 audit profile, then gemini-2.5-pro.

Targeted rescan

Primary: claude-opus-4-7. Fallback: gpt-5.5 roadmap profile, claude-sonnet-4-6, then gemini-2.5-pro.

Evidence check

Primary: gemini-3.1-pro-preview. Fallback: claude-sonnet-4-6, then gpt-5.5 evidence-check profile.

Synthesis and roadmap

Summary primary: claude-sonnet-4-6. Roadmap primary: claude-opus-4-7. Fallbacks: gpt-5.5 profiles and Gemini Pro-class models.

Fact-check and quality gate

Primary: gpt-5.5. Fallback: claude-sonnet-4-6 for fact-check and quality-gate explanation; high fact-check uses gpt-5.5 high reasoning first.

10 Why It Matters

The Difference Between Tool Adoption and Value Creation

A generic AI-readiness survey asks whether the organization has a strategy, tools, pilots, data, and governance. The AI Transformation Engine asks whether the documents prove that those things operate together as a value-creation system.

Tool Adoption Without Readinessfalse positive
A chatbot rollout, model gateway, or pilot portfolio can look advanced while decision rights, data context, release control, service ownership, and value measurement are still missing.
Governance Without Enablementcontrol theater
Policies and approvals do not prove safe value creation unless controls are embedded into workflows, architecture, data access, release lifecycle, and escalation paths.
Pilots Without Absorptionpilot purgatory
A pilot is useful only when it tests value, safety, integration, adoption, learning, and the transition from Kickstart to Building the System.
Data Without Meaningcontext failure
Data becomes fuel only when it carries semantic and service context. Raw availability is not the same as usable AI grounding.
Architecture Without Ownershipscaling friction
AI cannot scale safely when service boundaries, ownership, platform capabilities, and human accountability are unclear.
The differentiator is not “AI readiness.” The differentiator is evidence-gated proof that the organization has the operating, architectural, data, governance, and service structures required for AI to create measurable value safely.
